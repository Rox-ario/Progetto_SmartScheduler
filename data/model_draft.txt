[1. SCHEDULING HORIZON]
- Start Date: 7th December 2026
- End Date: 6th January 2027
- Total Days: 31 days (Index 0 to 30)

[2. AVAILABLE WORKERS]
- Total Workers (N): 13
- Worker IDs: 0 to N-1

[3. AVAILABLE SHIFTS PER DAY]
Each day includes three shifts. The 'workload/shift weight' is based on duration:
- Shift 0 (Morning): 08:00 - 14:00 (Duration: 6h, Shift Weight: 1)
- Shift 1 (Afternoon): 14:00 - 20:00 (Duration: 6h, Shift Weight: 1)
- Shift 2 (Night): 20:00 - 08:00 (Duration: 12h, Shift Weight: 2)

[4. HARD CONSTRAINTS (LEGAL & SCHEDULING REQUIREMENTS)]

HC1_MAX_HOURS_PER_WEEK:
- Description: Each employee's working time cannot exceed 36 hours per week.
- Logic: In any rolling 7-day window, the sum of hours worked (6 for Morning/Afternoon, 12 for Night) by a single worker must be <= 36.

HC2_MONTHLY_WORKLOAD:
- Description: Each worker must cover exactly 25 shifts in a month.
- Logic: The sum of "Shift Weights" (Morning=1, Afternoon=1, Night=2) for each worker across the 31-day horizon must be exactly equal to 25.

HC3_NIGHT_SHIFT_REST:
- Description: It is mandatory to ensure each employee two free days after each night shift.
- Logic: If a worker is assigned Shift 2 (Night) on Day D, they must have 0 shifts assigned on Day D+1 and Day D+2.

HC4_MAX_ONE_SHIFT_PER_DAY:
- Description: Each worker can cover up to one shift per day.
- Logic: The sum of boolean assignments for Shift 0, 1, and 2 for a specific worker on a specific day must be <= 1.

HC5_NO_SUBSEQUENT_SHIFTS:
- Description: A worker cannot cover two subsequent shifts.
- Logic: Prevents back-to-back shift assignments. Specifically, a worker cannot do Shift 1 (Afternoon) on Day D and Shift 0 (Morning) on Day D+1. (Note: Night shift transitions are already covered and restricted by HC3).

HC6_MANDATORY_WEEKLY_REST:
- Description: Each worker must be ensured a day of rest.
- Logic: In any consecutive 7-day block, every worker must have at least one completely free day (0 shifts assigned). Workers can express preferences on WHICH day this should be via Soft Constraints.
